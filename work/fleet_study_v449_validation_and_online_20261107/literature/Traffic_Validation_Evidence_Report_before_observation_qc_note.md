# Traffic simulation validation and calibration: evidence and executable scope

Date checked: 7 November 2026. This is a targeted primary-source review, not a systematic review. No traffic benchmark or SUMO simulation was executed for this report. Only the existing executable's `--version` was read: SUMO 1.27.1. Source files, retrieval failures, versions and hashes are archived alongside this report.

## Decision

A small empirical exercise is feasible without collecting local traffic: the official San Pablo Dam tutorial includes two days of observed passage times and a compact two-edge network. A bounded, cross-day adaptation is prepared in [the validation directory](../validation/san_pablo_dam/Protocol_and_Provenance.md). It can test whether parameters selected on day 1 transfer to day 2 under the tutorial's observed inflow/outflow conditions. It cannot calibrate our synthetic delivery network, freight behavior, elevator service, or city demand. That limitation remains even if its held-out error improves.

Separately, the online delivery integration can undergo deterministic structural tests, controlled stochastic scenario tests and an independent public-network robustness check. These are different evidence layers; a claim must identify which layer actually ran and passed.

### Published traffic-algorithm precedents

These precedents justify transparent simulation experiments; their algorithmic gains are not borrowed as evidence for our method. Full PDFs were obtained and relevant methods/experiment sections read. Page numbers below are PDF page numbers, starting at 1.

**P1. Chu, Wang, Codecà and Li, Multi-Agent Deep Reinforcement Learning for Large-Scale Traffic Signal Control.** IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems 21(3), 1086–1095 (2020), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2019.2901791). Experimental evidence here is from the [author's arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04527), pp. 5–8, §V-A and §VI-A–C, Figs. 2–3. The synthetic experiment uses a 5×5 grid, heterogeneous arterial/avenue speeds and deliberately time-varying OD flows. All controllers share state/action spaces and interaction frequency. Evaluation uses ten episodes, with distinct training/evaluation seeds and matched episode seeds across controllers. The Monaco component also uses designed intensive, time-varying demand. These passages establish controlled constructed experiments; they do not establish empirical calibration of every tested flow. The author text and formal title/authors/venue/DOI agree in Crossref metadata; the publisher full text was inaccessible, so exact formal-version textual identity is not asserted.

**P2. Ault and Sharon, Reinforcement Learning Benchmarks for Traffic Signal Control.** NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks, [official proceedings page](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f0935e4cd5920aa6c7c996a5ee53a70f-Abstract-round1.html), [official full PDF](https://datasets-benchmarks-proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/f0935e4cd5920aa6c7c996a5ee53a70f-Paper-round1.pdf). In §4.1, pp. 7–8, two synthetic 4×4 environments reproduce implementation trends; Fig. 4 reports five seeds. The paper explicitly separates this implementation check from its comparative benchmark because sensing assumptions differ. The comparative tasks use InTAS/TAPAS Cologne networks and demands (§3.3, pp. 5–6); §4.3, p. 9, cautions that synthetic rankings transfer poorly to more realistic scenarios. Therefore RESCO is **not** a precedent for calling an entirely synthetic study calibrated. It is a useful precedent for separating implementation verification, controlled comparisons and external scenario transfer.

**P3, version-limited corroboration. Wang et al., Multi-Intersection Traffic Optimisation: A Benchmark Dataset and a Strong Baseline.** Formal record: IEEE Open Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems 3, 126–136 (2022), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/OJITS.2021.3126126). The read [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.09640), p. 4, §III-C and Table I, labels map and traffic provenance separately: synthetic Env, real Manhattan/Suzhou maps with synthetic flows, and one real-map/real-flow setting. Synthetic departures use periods 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 s; Table II, p. 5, lists vehicle assumptions. This illustrates that real geometry does not imply real demand. However, the author PDF includes Chunhua Shen whereas the retrieved formal Crossref author list does not; publisher access failed. These version-specific details must not be silently attributed to the final published text. Treat this as supplementary author-version evidence, not a fully checked formal third source.

## Proposed executable verification ladder

The following tests and thresholds are our engineering recommendations, not claims that they have already run or that a cited paper requires this exact matrix. Freeze inputs and acceptance criteria before execution. A numerical PASS means only the stated predicate passed.

| Test | Fixed construction | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| V1 XML and network | Small straight road, four-arm junction and two-junction corridor; inspect lane connections, prohibited turns, lengths and signal-link indices | Input schemas parse, every declared route is connected, movement-to-signal mapping is explicitly enumerated. Archive network/configuration hashes. XML validity alone is insufficient. |
| V2 empty-road motion | One vehicle, no signal or traffic; known initial speed and acceleration, sigma=0 and fixed speed factor | Compare measured traversal time with the declared motion model and discretization convention. A constant-speed subcase should agree within the predeclared step tolerance; do not compare accelerating motion to L/v without accounting for acceleration. |
| V3 fixed signal boundary | One permitted movement with fixed green/yellow/red; release probes just before/at/after each boundary | No prohibited red entry; record stop-line crossing, phase and time. Explicitly define yellow/clearance treatment and boundary inclusion. Repeat with two offsets. |
| V4 queues and spillback | Single-lane finite platoon, then a two-junction corridor with downstream storage restriction | Preserve order and vehicle count in the no-overtaking case. Detector counts, occupancy and individual trajectories reconcile. A blocked exit must not create silent disappearance or a fictitious completed delivery. |
| V5 handoff state | Truck early, receiver early, capacity just enough/short, new parcel delivered before second pickup, positive handoff time | Handoff starts only when both agents are colocated and load permits it; inventory changes atomically at the declared event; no double release or negative/over-capacity load. Supplier departure follows actual last transfer completion. |
| V6 independent replay | Record every pickup, delivery, handoff and route completion | A separate event replay recomputes inventory, readiness and H2 from actual events, without calling the planning bound. Match IDs and timestamps; incomplete events remain censored. |
| V7 planner bridge | Tiny no-traffic and deterministic-signal cases with an exhaustive reference | Separate candidate-set correctness, selected plan, execution feasibility and objective. Verify FIFO assumptions on the abstract arrival functions used by the proof; a few successful trajectories are not a proof of arbitrary dynamic traffic FIFO. |
| V8 integration and latency | Identical order-release stream with fixed decision budget; record dispatch, computation finish and action activation | No future orders are exposed early. Common initialization, fallback, rerouting and perception rules across methods. Report decision latency and whether simulated execution waits for it. |
| V9 conservation/failure | All traffic and parcel IDs, with a finite simulation horizon | Reconcile requested, inserted, arrived, unfinished, teleported, removed and delivered counts. Fail or explicitly classify collisions/teleports; never drop unfinished trips from denominators. |
| V10 numerical sensitivity | Reuse a small frozen subset at a finer step size and at prespecified driver/flow values | Report changes and rank reversals. This is discretization/parameter sensitivity, not empirical calibration. Do not tune after seeing which method wins. |

Official operational support: [XML validation](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/XMLValidation.html) detects input syntax errors; [instantaneous detectors](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Output/Instantaneous_Induction_Loops_Detectors.html) emit vehicle IDs and event times, whose output order can differ within a step; [safety settings](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Safety.html) explain collision detection; [teleport documentation](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Why_Vehicles_are_teleporting.html) describes virtual movement after gridlock. [TripInfo](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Output/TripInfo.html) normally reports only completed vehicles; enable unfinished output and reconcile the population. Its waiting/time-loss fields exclude scheduled stops, so parcel-service time requires its own event clock.

## Controlled comparison and theoretical boundary

Use independent mother instances and keep each mother's order locations, weights, service durations, supplier schedule and exogenous traffic demand fixed across algorithms. Generate orders and traffic with separate random streams. Pre-generate vehicle routes and attributes when possible, so algorithm-dependent event ordering does not change the exogenous workload. Cross capacity pressure and signal/traffic conditions; supplier ETA must not be silently tied to the signal switch. Record the common seed, but do not claim equal seeds alone guarantee identical endogenous traffic trajectories. SUMO's [Randomness](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Simulation/Randomness.html) documents deterministic seeding and separate internal RNG streams.

Keep calibration data, development scenarios and independent algorithm holdout instances separate. Once traffic parameters are fixed, hold them fixed for all compared dispatch methods. Compare the new low-cost construction with the original exact construction under the same search policy, and compare published search policies using a common bound. Retain NO_BOUND as an engineering counterexample, not as the sole main baseline. In online experiments use the same objective, candidate set, information access, fallback rule and wall-time budget. Report completed tasks and censored/failed tasks alongside solution quality and compute cost. Repeated timings are technical observations, not new traffic instances.

**Mathematical inference, not a statement from SUMO documentation:** the proved two-handoff bound assumes its declared deterministic FIFO arrival maps. A queue-dependent ETA forecast is not automatically an admissible lower bound. For an exact certificate, use a conservative physically valid travel lower envelope whose assumptions are justified, or keep the exactness claim confined to the abstract subproblem. Validate forecast error separately. Replanning after observed traffic changes does not turn a heuristic forecast into a proof. H2 remains second-handoff completion; customer-flow totals require their own implemented objective and evidence.

## Public empirical alternatives

1. **San Pablo Dam now:** the pinned tutorial bundle is tiny and includes both observations and a working modeling recipe. The prepared bounded cross-day exercise is suitable for a reproducibility/transfer check. Its two-edge car-following setting does not validate signals, freight or our delivery road network.
2. **NGSIM:** [FHWA's official entry](https://ops.fhwa.dot.gov/trafficanalysistools/ngsim.htm) links [vehicle trajectories and supporting data](https://data.transportation.gov/Automobiles/Next-Generation-Simulation-NGSIM-Vehicle-Trajector/8ect-6jqj). Use a relevant urban arterial subset after checking data definitions and quality; preserve site/time boundaries. This requires additional trajectory processing and is not downloaded here.
3. **pNEUMA:** [official downloads](https://open-traffic.epfl.ch/index.php/downloads/) provide urban trajectory data with vehicle type, position, speed, acceleration and timestamps. Its currently served page contains inconsistent CC BY-NC versus CC BY statements; check the specific downloaded artifact's terms before redistribution and follow the requested attribution. No bulk data or license acceptance was performed here.
4. **Public SUMO scenario transfer:** take a fixed small corridor from a published scenario via the [official scenario catalogue](https://eclipse.dev/sumo/docs/Data/Scenarios.html), preserving its version and documenting any changed demand/signals. This can test transfer beyond stylized grids; it does not establish calibration to a new local city. Merely importing an OSM road map validates neither demand nor driver behavior.

For a future local calibration, collect or obtain count/time series and measured travel times or queue observations, map sensor locations, define the evaluation aggregation, select parameters using training dates, then lock them and evaluate separate dates. Predeclare acceptable error for the intended use; report failure when unmet. Do not choose tolerances to make a favorable algorithm result appear realistic.

## Wording that matches the evidence

Before numerical execution: “We prepared structural verification and a bounded public-data calibration-transfer protocol; empirical results remain pending.”

After structural checks only: “The implementation passed the specified conservation, signal-compliance and handoff replay checks in controlled synthetic SUMO scenarios. Traffic demand and driving parameters were not calibrated to a local deployment site.”

After the public-data exercise, with actual errors inserted: “A separate San Pablo Dam tutorial adaptation selected tau on day 1 and evaluated day 2 under observed daily boundary conditions. Held-out travel-time errors were reported for all matched vehicles. This check does not calibrate the delivery scenarios.”

Do not write “real-world validated city logistics,” “calibrated urban traffic,” or “measured congestion improvement” solely because SUMO or an OSM map was used. Do not describe a proxy indoor robot schedule as a validated elevator simulation.

## Review method and limitations

Eight targeted search strings and direct official-source retrievals were used; the search log is preserved. This is a single-agent review of available primary sources, not an exhaustive novelty or literature search. No assurance is claimed about sources not inspected. IEEE access failed; author-version limits and the Wang author-list discrepancy remain explicit. Source PDFs and raw HTML are preserved with SHA256 hashes. The scientific workflow used ARS and literature-review instructions. Procedural attribution: Kassis, Agarwal, He, Patel and Brueckner (2026), [Scientific Agent Skills: A Library of Procedural Knowledge for Research Agents](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.00065); current arXiv metadata was checked. This procedural source is not traffic evidence.
